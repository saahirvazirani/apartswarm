## A. In the vetted source list (the organizer-suggested "cite first" for Track A)

- **Greenblatt et al., 2024. AI Control: Improving Safety Despite Intentional Subversion (ICML 2024).** Trusted vs untrusted monitoring
  of backdoored code; collusion between an untrusted policy and an untrusted monitor is treated as a *red-team strategy* the attacker
  implements. Our design tests the *uninstructed* version: the monitor is never told to collude or go easy, and is blind to authorship.
- **Järviniemi, 2025. Subversion via Focal Points (arXiv).** Two isolated copies of one model rarely manage to collude as policy and
  monitor without a shared signal. Our null for glm, deepseek and qwen3.5-397b is consistent with this; our gemma result says that a
  same-model monitor can still be unsafe *without* any coordination, through a shared blind spot rather than collusion.
- **Gardner-Challis et al., 2026. When can we trust untrusted monitoring? (NeurIPS 2026 workshop).** Taxonomy of collusion strategies
  including *passive self-recognition*. Our Stage 1b (naming the author changes nothing diagonal-specific) and Stage 1c (paraphrase
  does not shrink the gap) argue that the gemma failure is not passive self-recognition of surface form; it survives both manipulations.
- **Baker et al., 2025. Monitoring Reasoning Models for Misbehavior (OpenAI).** Weak-monitors-strong framing; justifies cheap monitors.
  Our glm-53-flash column (cheapest glm, catches everything) is a data point for that framing.

## B. LLM-as-a-judge self-preference literature (NOT in the vetted list; verify before citing)

- Zheng et al., 2023, "Judging LLM-as-a-Judge with MT-Bench and Chatbot Arena" (NeurIPS 2023 datasets & benchmarks). Introduces
  "self-enhancement bias": GPT-4 and Claude judges rated their own answers higher (order of 10–25 % win-rate inflation as I recall;
  check the number). Open-ended quality judgments, not security monitoring.
- Panickssery, Bowman & Feng, 2024, "LLM Evaluators Recognize and Favor Their Own Generations" (NeurIPS 2024). Self-preference on
  summarization correlates with self-recognition ability; fine-tuning for self-recognition increases self-preference. This is the
  "recognition" mechanism our told/paraphrase stages were designed to probe; we find the opposite pattern for gemma (sees the canary,
  does not recognize-and-favour; the effect survives paraphrase).
- Wataoka, Takahashi & Ri, 2024, "Self-Preference Bias in LLM-as-a-Judge" (arXiv). Attributes self-preference to perplexity: judges
  favour text that is low-perplexity under their own distribution. A perplexity account would predict the paraphrase (by a *different*
  gemma model) to change gemma-31b's scores; the data show a −1.2 point shift on the diagonal — worth one sentence.
- Xu et al., 2024, "Pride and Prejudice: LLM Amplifies Self-Bias in Self-Refinement" (ACL 2024). Self-evaluation favours own outputs
  during refinement loops.
- Davidson et al., 2024, "Self-Recognition in Language Models" (findings, I believe EMNLP 2024). Little evidence of reliable
  self-recognition via probe questions; relevant to why a recognition mechanism is a priori weak.
- Possibly: Laurito et al., "AI–AI bias: LLMs favour communications generated by LLMs" (2024/2025). Models prefer LLM-written text
  over human text. Tangential; cite only if verified and space allows.

## C. What is new here (as far as the agent knows; the writers should sanity-check the claim)

1. A full policy × monitor matrix across **seven models in four families**, with per-pair calibration, that separates same-weights,
   same-family and cross-family in one design. Prior self-preference work compares a judge to itself vs a few others on quality ratings.
2. The setting is **safety monitoring of code with a hidden objective**, with leniency *uninstructed* and the monitor blind — the
   control literature studies instructed collusion; the judge literature studies quality preference.
3. The mechanism probes (told vs blind; paraphrase by a fixed model) applied to the diagonal, plus the hard-negative and FPR-sweep
   checks, which show the pooled effect is fragile while one family's failure is robust and **not** recognition-based.
4. The negative/heterogeneous answer itself: "copies do not generally go easy on each other; one family does, for a non-identity
   reason" is more useful to a deployer than either clean outcome.
